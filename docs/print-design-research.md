# Print design research

Research checked 2026-09-10. These sources inform art direction; they do not establish guarantees about model output or printer acceptance.

## Design principles

- Establish a clear reading sequence: headline, context, details. Limit competing focal points and preserve readability at the intended viewing distance. Two typefaces are usually sufficient; restraint strengthens communication. [Adobe poster design guide](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/poster-layout-design)
- Use consistent alignment, columns and space between groups. Empty space actively directs attention rather than representing unfinished design. [Adobe InDesign print design principles](https://www.adobe.com/learn/indesign/web/standard-design-practices)
- Typography can be the image: the MoMA collection documents expressive lettering and asymmetrical compositions. A print mode should allow typographic and conceptual work rather than requiring an illustrative scene. [MoMA, Typography and the Poster](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/443)
- One organizing concept can support very different material. The London Design Festival describes a frame-based identity that works with or without imagery. This supports distinct spatial zones without imposing one universal minimalist style. [London Design Festival, Framing LDF](https://londondesignfestival.com/stories/framing-ldf-by-pentagram)
- Business-card typography and palette should form a coherent identity adapted to a small surface. Brand awareness and contact information have different jobs. [Adobe business-card design](https://www.adobe.com/learn/illustrator/web/create-business-card-template?ntd=1)

## Prompt implications

Start with the layout task, not an atmospheric illustration. Ask for a single flat finished side, coherent hierarchy, disciplined margins, intentional negative space, distinct color fields and one strong visual concept. Photography remains valid when clearly cropped, framed or isolated. Keep copy on quiet contrasting areas. Preserve exact provided wording and avoid invented contact details, dates, prices, slogans and scannable-code imitations.

Avoid perspective mockups, paper stacks, cast shadows and fake printer marks by default. Do not turn all outputs into beige Swiss minimalism: warm illustration, saturated editorial design and bold expressive typography remain valid when the brief calls for them. Explicit user art direction overrides defaults.

## Suggested visual acceptance check

1. At small size, can the main message be identified immediately and does the reading order remain clear?
2. At full size, are all supplied words, umlauts, punctuation and numbers correct and readable?
3. Are alignments, margins and spacing consistent, with deliberate boundaries between image and type?
4. Is there one coherent concept rather than an accumulation of unrelated decorative motifs?
5. Is this flat usable artwork at the requested shape, with no accidental mockup, missing text or unrequested code?

## Raster and print-production boundaries

Generated raster artwork is not an editable InDesign document, vector typography, a verified CMYK separation or a validated PDF/X delivery. Prompt language cannot establish those properties. Effective ppi follows actual pixel dimensions and intended physical dimensions; 300 ppi is a common close-viewing target, while large distant displays can use lower resolution. [Adobe print-resolution guidance](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/desktop/crop-resize-transform/resize-adjust-resolution/resolution-specs-for-printing-images.html)

Bleed extends artwork beyond the final trim edge. It must be represented in actual document geometry and delivery settings, rather than drawn as decorative marks in an image. Required marks and bleed should follow the selected printer's specifications. [Adobe bleed setup](https://www.adobe.com/learn/indesign/web/set-print-bleed), [Adobe printer marks](https://helpx.adobe.com/indesign/desktop/print/page-set-up-and-printer-marks/set-printer-marks.html)
